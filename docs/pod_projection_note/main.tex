\section{POD representation and projection}

The POD construction follows the linear PDF-model idea of
Ref.~\cite{LinearPDF}. At $Q_0=1.65\,\mathrm{GeV}$, member zero of the
LHAPDF set \texttt{250503\_pod\_basis\_40k} is the reference PDF
$f_a^{(0)}$. The 100 non-reference members define the shifts
\begin{equation}
  B_{ai}(x,Q) = f_a^{(i)}(x,Q)-f_a^{(0)}(x,Q),
  \qquad i=1,\ldots,100.
\end{equation}
Here and below all PDF values mean $xf_a$. A POD PDF is therefore
\begin{equation}
  f_a^{\mathrm{POD}}(x,Q_0;\boldsymbol c)=f_a^{(0)}(x,Q_0)
       +\sum_{i=1}^{100}B_{ai}(x,Q_0)c_i.
\end{equation}
QCDNUM evolves the PDF represented by this input condition; the numerical
coefficients $c_i$ themselves are scale independent \cite{QCDNUM}.

\subsection{Design matrix}

For a target PDF $t_a(x,Q_{\rm ext})$, the basis and target are always
evaluated at the same $Q_{\rm ext}$. In the Figure--2 implementation the
rows are indexed by the ordered pair $(a,k)$, with
\begin{equation}
  a=(g,u,\bar u,d,\bar d,s,\bar s,c,\bar c,b,\bar b)
\end{equation}
and $x_k$ the 160 retained LHAPDF nodes,
$1.166\times10^{-5}\leq x_k\leq1$. Thus the basic design matrix has
$11\times160=1760$ rows and 100 columns:
\begin{equation}
  X_{(a,k),i}=B_{ai}(x_k,Q_{\rm ext}),
  \qquad
  d_{(a,k)}=t_a(x_k,Q_{\rm ext})-f_a^{(0)}(x_k,Q_{\rm ext}).
\end{equation}
In other words, its $i$th column is the full flavour-and-$x$ supervector of
the shift member $i$ minus member zero. All basis elements means members 1
through 100; a truncated model simply retains the requested subset of those
columns. The generic CT18NNLO validation uses these native columns, with no
amplitude rescaling and an absolute metric.

\subsection{Minimisation}

For the basic objective, the coefficients are the weighted linear least-squares
solution
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\boldsymbol c}=\underset{\boldsymbol c}{\operatorname{argmin}}
  \;\left\|W^{1/2}(X\boldsymbol c-\boldsymbol d)\right\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
For a diagonal $W$, the formal normal equations are
\begin{equation}
  (X^T W X)\hat{\boldsymbol c}=X^T W\boldsymbol d.
\end{equation}
The CMS-specific implementation does \emph{not} explicitly invert this
normal matrix. It stacks the absolute rows and all additional weighted rows
into an augmented system $A\boldsymbol c\simeq\boldsymbol b$, then calls
\texttt{numpy.linalg.lstsq}, i.e. a rank-revealing LAPACK least-squares solve.
There is no bound, prior, regularisation, or experimental likelihood in this
step. The returned PDF is $f^{(0)}+X\hat{\boldsymbol c}$.

On this local machine, constructing the 1760-by-100 operator, including
reading and evaluating all 101 LHAPDF members, took 6.3~s. Once built, a
full Figure--2 projection took 9~ms (average of 20 calls). The projection is
therefore negligible compared with an xFitter likelihood evaluation; the
operator should be built once and reused for all scan points.
\section{Modifications for CMS Inclusive jets (13 TeV)}
The retained CMS objective supplements the absolute residual with relative
closure terms:
\begin{align}
  \chi^2_{\rm proj} ={}& \|X\boldsymbol c-\boldsymbol d\|_2^2
  + \lambda_g^2
    \sum_{0.05<x<0.99}
    \left(\frac{r_g}{\max(|t_g|,\epsilon)}\right)^2
    \nonumber\\
  &+ \lambda_v^2
    \sum_{10^{-4}<x<0.1}\sum_{q=u,d}
    \left(\frac{r_{q-\bar q}}
    {\max(|t_{q-\bar q}|,\epsilon)}\right)^2
    \nonumber\\
  &+ \lambda_{F_2}^2
    \sum_{10^{-4}<x<0.1}
    \left(\frac{r_{F_2}}
    {\max(|t_{F_2}|,\epsilon)}\right)^2.
\end{align}
Here $r$ denotes target minus reconstruction, and
\begin{equation}
  F_2^{\rm proxy}=
  \sum_{q=u,d,s,c,b}e_q^2(q+\bar q).
\end{equation}
The implementation appends one row to $A$ for every term in these sums,
dividing both the corresponding row of $X$ and component of $d$ by its
denominator and multiplying both by $\lambda$. Consequently, it is exactly
the same linear least-squares problem described above. The production
settings are $\lambda_g=0.1$, $\lambda_v=0.1$, and $\lambda_{F_2}=3$.
They are projection objectives only---neither experimental likelihood terms
nor PDF priors.

The relative high-$x$ gluon term is the \emph{gluon-tail enhancement}. An absolute norm otherwise underweights a small but phenomenologically relevant high-$x$ gluon. The valence and $F_2$ terms protect low-$x$ DIS-relevant light-quark combinations.

The direct HERAPDF input starts at $\sqrt{1.9}\,\mathrm{GeV}$ while POD
starts at 1.65~GeV. Valid closure therefore exports the direct PDF on a full
LHAPDF grid down to $x=10^{-9}$, reimports it at 1.65~GeV, and evolves both
inputs through the same QCDNUM route. This prevents grid, input-scale, and
evolution-history effects from being called POD compression. The
implementation is in xFitter \cite{XFitter}, with the CMS/HERA configuration
supplied alongside the CMS analysis \cite{CMS,HERA}.

Pointwise high-$x$ $u,\bar u,d,\bar d$ relative terms were rejected because vanishing antiquarks are ill conditioned. Stable high-$x$ valence/$F_2$ trials also did not improve closure. The full basis spans the light combinations but only along a highly ill-conditioned direction that catastrophically damages gluon closure; these trials are diagnostics, not production settings.

\section{From projected coefficients to the CMS likelihood}
The projection returns only the 100-dimensional vector $\hat{\boldsymbol c}$.
It is not itself a prediction or a fit to data.  To evaluate the likelihood of
the corresponding PDF, xFitter first reconstructs the complete set of input
flavours at $Q_0=1.65\,\mathrm{GeV}$ from the POD representation above, and evolves that input
with QCDNUM at NNLO \cite{XFitter,QCDNUM}.  The $c_i$ are held fixed during
this evolution: it is the reconstructed input PDF, rather than each
coefficient separately, that is evolved.  The value of $\alpha_s(m_Z)$ is
passed independently of the projection, since it is not encoded in the POD
coefficients.

For the CMS inclusive-jet data, the evolved PDFs and $\alpha_s$ are supplied
to the four NNLO fastNLO interpolation tables, one per measured rapidity bin.
Each table represents the NNLO partonic calculation, and the fastNLO toolkit
performs the PDF and $\alpha_s$ convolution for the trial PDF at
$\mu_R=\mu_F=p_T^{\rm jet}$ \cite{fastNLO}.  Thus no jet cross-section
variations in the POD directions are precomputed.  At every coefficient
vector, fastNLO obtains a new parton-level prediction $F_i(\hat{\boldsymbol
c})$ for each data bin.  The prediction entering the measurement is
\begin{equation}
  T_i(\hat{\boldsymbol c}) = F_i(\hat{\boldsymbol c})\,
       C^{\rm NP}_i\,C^{\rm EW}_i,
\end{equation}
where the tabulated non-perturbative and electroweak correction factors are
the same fixed factors as in the direct CMS fit.  In parallel, the same
QCDNUM-evolved PDF is passed to the neutral- and charged-current DIS
reactions for the HERA data.

Schematically, xFitter evaluates the correlated experimental objective as
\begin{equation}
 \chi^2(\hat{\boldsymbol c},\boldsymbol\beta) =
 [\boldsymbol D-\boldsymbol T(\hat{\boldsymbol c})
       -\Gamma(\hat{\boldsymbol c})\boldsymbol\beta]^T
 C^{-1}(\hat{\boldsymbol c})
 [\boldsymbol D-\boldsymbol T(\hat{\boldsymbol c})
       -\Gamma(\hat{\boldsymbol c})\boldsymbol\beta]
 +\boldsymbol\beta^T\boldsymbol\beta+\chi^2_{\rm log}.
\end{equation}
Here $\boldsymbol D$ denotes the measured HERA and CMS cross sections,
$C$ contains statistical and uncorrelated components (including the supplied
CMS statistical correlation matrix), $\Gamma$ contains the correlated
experimental shifts, and $\beta_k$ are unit-Gaussian Hessian nuisance
parameters.  The exact xFitter implementation uses the CMS analysis's
Poisson statistical scaling and linear multiplicative scaling of
uncorrelated and correlated uncertainties; consequently $C$ and $\Gamma$
can depend on the prediction.  The final term denotes the associated
logarithmic penalty.  This equation is a compact description of that
implemented prescription, rather than a replacement for the data cards.

For a likelihood-only evaluation, the reported POD likelihood is the
profiled value
\begin{equation}
  \chi^2_{\rm POD}(\hat{\boldsymbol c}) =
  \min_{\boldsymbol\beta}\chi^2(\hat{\boldsymbol c},\boldsymbol\beta).
\end{equation}
Therefore the experimental nuisance shifts are reprofiled after the PDF has
been projected.  In the POD-mediated analytic fit, an outer optimizer varies
the original 15 analytic PDF-shape parameters and $\alpha_s$; each trial
analytic PDF is exported at $1.65\,\mathrm{GeV}$, projected to
$\hat{\boldsymbol c}$ by the weighted least-squares construction, and assigned the profiled xFitter
likelihood above.  The POD coefficients are derived intermediate variables,
not additional fitted parameters.  The workflow is implemented by the
xFitter POD decomposition module, QCDNUM evolution, the fastNLO reaction,
and the projection driver in the accompanying analysis repository.

\section{Direct CMS analytic parametrisation}
At $Q_0^2=1.9\,\mathrm{GeV}^2$, the direct Figure--2 fit uses endpoint powers
times a low-order polynomial:
\begin{align}
  xg &= A_gx^{B_g}(1-x)^{C_g}(1+D_gx+E_gx^2),
  \\
  xu_v &= A_{u_v}x^{B_{u_v}}(1-x)^{C_{u_v}}(1+E_{u_v}x^2),
  \\
  xd_v &= A_{d_v}x^{B_{d_v}}(1-x)^{C_{d_v}},
  \\
  x\bar U &= A_{\bar U}x^{B_{\bar D}}(1-x)^{C_{\bar U}}
             (1+D_{\bar U}x),
  \\
  x\bar D &= A_{\bar D}x^{B_{\bar D}}(1-x)^{C_{\bar D}}
             (1+E_{\bar D}x^2).
\end{align}
The conventions are $\bar U=\bar u$, $\bar D=\bar d+\bar s$,
$x\bar d=(1-f_s)x\bar D$, $x\bar s=f_sx\bar D$, $f_s=0.4$, and
$A_{\bar U}=(1-f_s)A_{\bar D}$. Valence-number and momentum sum rules fix
$A_g$, $A_{u_v}$, and $A_{d_v}$. The 16 independent parameters are
\begin{align*}
  &B_g,C_g,D_g,E_g,B_{u_v},C_{u_v},E_{u_v},B_{d_v},C_{d_v},\\
  &A_{\bar D},B_{\bar D},C_{\bar D},E_{\bar D},C_{\bar U},D_{\bar U},
    \alpha_s(m_Z).
\end{align*}
\section{Other global-fit parametrisations}
\subsection{CT18NNLO}
CT18NNLO uses the CTEQ--TEA Hessian framework and low-scale endpoint powers times Bernstein-polynomial expansions \cite{CT18}, $xf_a=x^{a_{1,a}}(1-x)^{a_{2,a}}P_a(y)$, where $y$ is a monotonic map such as $\sqrt{x}$. Bernstein polynomials give local shape control; sum rules and flavour relations apply at $Q_0$; Hessian eigenvectors encode uncertainties. CT18NNLO is the target used by the generic POD validation.
\subsection{MSHT and NNPDF}
MSHT uses endpoint powers times flexible Chebyshev-polynomial expansions with Hessian uncertainties \cite{MSHT}. NNPDF uses neural networks with preprocessing factors $xf_a=x^{\alpha_a}(1-x)^{\beta_a}\mathrm{NN}_a(x)$ and Monte-Carlo replicas \cite{NNPDF}. All are input-scale parametrisations followed by DGLAP evolution. POD instead fixes shape functions from an ensemble and fits only linear coordinates.
\section{Scope}
The scale-aligned full 100-mode POD has small local likelihood differences from the direct HERA+CMS fit. This does not validate a five- or seven-mode fit; every truncation requires its own closure and minimised-fit validation.
